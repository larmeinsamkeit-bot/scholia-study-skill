## 1. Demand and supply

The **law of demand**: other things equal, when the price of a good rises, the quantity demanded falls.
A change in price moves us **along** the demand curve; a change in income, tastes, the prices of related
goods or expectations **shifts** the curve.

The **law of supply**: other things equal, when the price rises, the quantity supplied rises.

Market equilibrium is where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied.

Worked example: Qd = 100 − 2P and Qs = 20 + 2P. Setting them equal: 100 − 2P = 20 + 2P, so P* = 20
and Q* = 60.

## 2. Shifts and the opportunity cost link

If the price of a substitute rises, demand for the good shifts right. Remember from Week 1: what a
consumer gives up (opportunity cost) is what drives their choice between substitutes.

## 3. Price elasticity of demand

Elasticity measures how much quantity demanded responds to price:

  E = (% change in Qd) / (% change in P)

Use the **midpoint method**: % change = (new − old) / ((new + old) / 2).

Example: price rises from $4 to $6 and quantity falls from 120 to 80.
%ΔQ = −40 / 100 = −40%; %ΔP = 2 / 5 = 40%; |E| = 1 (unit elastic).

- |E| > 1: elastic — total revenue falls when price rises.
- |E| < 1: inelastic — total revenue rises when price rises.
